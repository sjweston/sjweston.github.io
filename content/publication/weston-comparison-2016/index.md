## Why we asked

Most research on life stories relies on trained human coders. Automated word-count tools, in contrast, are often used to study how people speak and write. We wanted to know whether software could stand in for human coders.

## What we did

We ran two studies. The first used in-person interviews (N = 158) and the second used life stories collected online (N = 242). In each, we compared human-coded redemption scores with the output of the Linguistic Inquiry and Word Count program (LIWC). We also looked for patterns of language with an exploratory principal components analysis.

## What we found

Redemption scores were modestly correlated with some LIWC categories and unassociated with the language components. The language patterns did not replicate across the two samples, so the structure we found in one did not carry over to the other. Redemption scores and the language components each predicted life satisfaction up to three years later, and they did so independently of one another.

## What it means

In other words, human coding and automated analysis were not redundant, and each added information that the other did not. We read them as complementary methods, not substitutes.
