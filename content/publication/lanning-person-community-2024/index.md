## What we did

Person-community fit is the match between a person and the people around them. Measuring it is harder than it sounds, because people within a community vary more than communities vary from each other. We compared three approaches using data from the Synthetic Aperture Personality Assessment project, which included 75,705 people across the US. The first two were variable-centered (response surface analysis) and person-centered (profile similarity). The third was a typological approach, in which people are sorted into personality types, communities are described by the mix of types they contain, and fit is the proportion of a community that shares your type. We then asked how fit relates to education, health, and well-being.

## What we found

Using a rudimentary typology based on the five-factor model, we found striking differences between communities in their type profiles. For both the profile and type approaches, fit was modestly associated with the outcomes. Most of that association reflected normativity, meaning person-country fit, and not what distinguishes one community from another. As we read it, resembling the typical American mattered more than resembling one's own neighbors.

Using more than one method gave us a fuller picture of both communities and fit.
